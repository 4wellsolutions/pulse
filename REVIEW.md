# PULSE — review

56.0 s, 24 fps, 12 shots, one canvas drawn in 2D from film time. Rendered files: `renders/pulse_9x16.mp4`
(1080 x 1920) and `renders/pulse_1x1.mp4` (1080 x 1080).

## Creative calls

- The hero is a line. The world is line art that exists only where the line has been: bright at its head, a blue
  trace behind it, a hint ahead of it, and a reflection on the wet street.
- The score is built on screen. Each place gives one rhythm and one coloured bead that follows the head of the line
  and pulses on its own hits. The fog takes the beads away one by one; the laugh brings them back.
- Everything that makes a sound moves on its hit: dhol sticks, the rope, sparks under the train wheels, roof
  splashes, wave spray. The rhythm patterns are the same numbers in the picture code and in the audio code.
- The line slows down where a rhythm is made (the dhol player, the skipping rope) and hurries between.
- Full colour exists once, in the hospital room after the first new beep.
- People move on twos. Impact frames (inverted, gold, white) on the spike at 39.0 s and the beep at 49.5 s.
- The turn was written as a newborn's cry. No free recording was found and a synthesized cry would sound fake, so
  it is a child's laugh (recorded).

## Measured, looked at, not checked

- Looked at: about 70 stills across all 12 shots in 9:16 and about 20 in 1:1, including both impact cuts.
- Sound: measured, not heard. Integrated loudness about -13.7 LUFS, loudness range 13.6 LU. Section levels (RMS):
  heartbeat alone -29 dBFS, with rain -23, with train -19, with dhol -15, with rope and sea -14, last fog bar -36,
  the run home -12, digital silence from 48.4 s to the beep at 49.5 s, dawn -19.
- Not checked: the film played at speed by the person who built it. Motion was judged from stills. The owner
  watched the 9:16 browser preview and approved it before the render.

## Weakest shots, and what more time would buy

- The people are simple line figures. The dhol player's drum is the least convincing drawing.
- The top third of the 9:16 frame is mostly empty night sky during the journey; the 1:1 frame is fuller.
- The skipping rope depends on motion to read; in a still it can look like a dip in the line.
- The instruments are made in code. The rhythm layers suit that; the short tune on the run home is the part most
  likely to sound thin.
- The run home passes each place in under a second. A longer cut (70 s) would let each rhythm land again.
