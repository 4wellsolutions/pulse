# Storylines

Reference: HITOFUDE 一筆 (github.com/shubhu121/hitdude), a 50 s ink-painting trailer drawn in code.
What we take from it: one canvas as a pure function of time, 24 fps, people on twos, impact frames, a written brief
before any drawing, an idea where the look and the story are the same thing, a title that is earned.
What we do differently: no enemy that erases the world. The film is about one line, and the music is built on screen.

## 1. PULSE (picked)

The hero is a line. A heart stops; the last blip leaves the monitor as one unbroken stroke of light, draws every
place it crosses, collects a rhythm in each, loses them in the fog, and comes home as a heartbeat.
- First three seconds: beep, beep, a late beep, flat.
- The art is the story: the world exists on screen only where the line has been.
- The music is the story: every place adds one layer, the fog takes them away, the run home plays them together.
- Look: night blue-black, one line that warms from green to gold, one colour per rhythm, full colour once (dawn).

## 2. LUMEN

The film is a stained-glass window. A glass knight fights the Crack, a black fracture that drops panes into
darkness; her sword is a lead line; sunrise through the window re-leads the pieces into a rose, which is the title.
Strong look, but it is the reference's own shape (a medium against the thing that destroys it).

## 3. STITCH

Embroidery on indigo cloth. A stitched girl with a needle for a spear fights the Unraveller, scissors that turn
mountains and birds into loose thread; one unbroken running stitch becomes a crane; the last knot is the title.

## 4. RANG

The painted panel of a truck. A painted falcon peels off the metal and fights Rust along a night highway; at dawn
it repaints the last panel with its wingtip and the brush-lettered slogan on the back is the title.

## 5. THE STAR CATCHER (sampled, set aside)

A fox cub whose mother is a constellation: her heart-star falls, he fetches it through wheat, swamp, forest and
storm, and puts it back. Three 30 s samples were built; the look improved each time but the code-made orchestral
score did not convince. PULSE came out of that: lead with rhythm, which code can make well.
